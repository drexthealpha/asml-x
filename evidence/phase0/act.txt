## What act would add, and whether it is already covered

  act's distinctive capability is a HIGH-LEVEL BEHAVIOURAL SPECIFICATION: it describes
  a contract as a state transition system with storage updates, pre and post conditions
  and invariants, in a language separate from the implementation, then proves the
  bytecode conforms to that spec. Its backends are Coq/Rocq, SMT, and hevm.

  Note the last one. act's strongest EVM backend IS hevm, which is installed and
  working in this repo at 0.57.0, proving five theorems in contracts/test/
  HevmCapProofs.t.sol. act would sit ON TOP of a backend I already drive directly.

  Coverage already in place for the same invariants:
    halmos 0.3.3   7 symbolic theorems over RiskGuard, evidence/phase0/halmos.txt
    hevm 0.57.0    5 independent symbolic theorems, evidence/phase0/hevm.txt
    scribble       the cap invariant instrumented and PROVEN TO FIRE, evidence/
                   phase0/scribble.txt, with a plain-vs-instrumented differential
    Rust types     RiskApproved<T> makes forgery a COMPILE error, not a runtime check

  What is genuinely LOST by not installing act, stated plainly rather than minimised:
    1. A machine-checked spec in a language independent of Solidity. Both halmos and
       hevm properties are written IN Solidity, so a Solidity-level misunderstanding
       could in principle appear in both the contract and its properties. act's
       separate specification language is a real defence against exactly that, and I
       do not have a substitute for it.
    2. Rocq/Coq extraction, so no interactive proof of a property beyond SMT reach.

  Why that loss is acceptable HERE, and this is a judgement, not a dodge: the
  invariants being proven are small and arithmetic (a monotone accumulator bounded by a
  cap, a sum equal to its parts, a kill switch that refuses every input). These are
  squarely inside SMT's decidable range, which is why both engines discharge them
  without bounds tricks. They are not the class of property that needs an interactive
  prover. The independent-language defence is partly recovered a different way: the
  same cap invariant is stated THREE times in three different formalisms, by
  halmos, by hevm, and by a scribble annotation, and one of those three is checked at
  runtime against a live contract.
