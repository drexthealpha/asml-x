# Density, measured at 1920x1080

Task 4.1. The PASS condition is "largest empty rectangle at 1920x1080 is at or below the 3.9
baseline". This file reports what was measured, including the part that does not meet it.

Reproduce: serve the built UI and run `scripts/measure-density.js` in the browser console at a
1920x1080 viewport.

```bash
bash scripts/78-ui-data.sh                 # stage the agent's real output files
cd ui-v2 && pnpm build && pnpm vite preview --host 127.0.0.1 --port 4173
# then, in the console at 1920x1080, paste scripts/measure-density.js
```

## The metric, and a measurement bug worth recording

"Largest empty axis-aligned rectangle as a percentage of the viewport." It matches what a reader
notices: one large void, not scattered gaps.

The first implementation marked any element with a background colour as occupied. Panel backgrounds
cover the whole screen, so it reported **100% occupied and a largest empty rectangle of zero**,
which measures nothing and would have passed the gate while proving the opposite. The working
version marks only the bounding rects of real text runs (`Range.getClientRects`) plus the gradient
bars, because density for a reader is about where the information is.

Step 5 traded 0.2 points of empty rectangle for a panel that displays the journal's `evidence`
array, which nothing in the v1 UI showed. That is a deliberate trade and it is recorded as one
rather than presented as an improvement.

Final state: **ink coverage 59.83%, largest empty rectangle 5.92% of the viewport**, 366 numeric
cells, 615 text runs, 54 gradient bars, seven panels.

## Step 6 is the interesting one: the same layout, real data

Step 6 changed **no CSS and no component**. It ran the agent for 40 cycles, which wrote 43 fresh
journal rows with 39 submitted transactions and 280 real limit refusals. Ink coverage went from
46.63% to 59.83% and the largest empty rectangle from 8.10% to 5.92%.

That is the whole argument for the layout being right and the data being thin: the panels were
already sized for content they did not have. It also means the earlier 8.10% was diagnosing a
missing build step, not a design flaw, and treating it as a design flaw would have led to padding
the screen with decoration.

Two other things step 6 fixed, both recorded elsewhere and both closed by the same run:

- The risk panel now draws a REAL utilisation bar: `MarketNotionalTooLarge 52.96 / 50.00 100%`,
  which is task 4.5's PASS condition ("at least one utilisation bar visibly near its limit during
  the demo run, otherwise the panel is decoration"). Exposure accumulated about 2.3 quote units per
  take against a 50-unit per-market cap, so the cap began binding after roughly 22 takes. Nothing
  was staged to make that happen.
- Zero wei-scaled values and zero baseline control rows in the staged journal:
  `bash scripts/77-journal-scale-audit.sh` now reports PASS with 280 refusals carrying usable
  numbers.

## Step 7: the single-page assumption was the actual defect

Everything above fights the same losing battle: eight panels on one 1920x1080 surface, reshuffled
after each measurement to move a void somewhere less costly. That is optimising a layout that should
not exist. HypeTerminal does not put its whole product on one surface. It tabs alternative views of
the same question (`orderbook-panel.tsx:87-101`) and routes between whole workspaces. The study cited
that pattern and the layout ignored it.

Three views now, driven by the tab bar or the digit keys 1 to 3:

| view | contents | ink | largest empty rect | numeric cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Decide** | Live brain, Evidence, Decision journal | **66.19%** | 624x120, **5.20%** | 418 |
| **2 Risk** | Refusal ledger, Risk, Decision journal | **69.58%** | 64x848, **3.77%** | 470 |
| **3 Chain** | Onchain metrics, Learning, Contracts, Transactions | **51.52%** | 552x136, **5.21%** | 193 |

Worst case across all three views: **5.21%**. Mean ink coverage: **62.43%**. Measured at 1600x900.
The Risk view is at **3.77%**, inside the 3.9% baseline.

### Tabs alone made it WORSE, and that is the useful part

First split into four views measured: Decide 26.29%, Risk 40.95%, Chain 12.13%, Learn 25.65%. Every
view got sparser, because the panels were the same and each view now had two or three of them. The
void was never about arrangement. It was that the UI was not showing data it already had.

What closed it, in order, each verified by re-measuring:

1. **The refusal ledger.** The Risk view was 41% empty while the journal held **2,236 refusals** the
   UI displayed only as totals. Rendering them as rows, with the got/limit numbers parsed from the
   engine's own refusal text, took Risk from 40.95% to 14.60% and ink from 33.06% to 57.01%.
2. **Four views became three.** Learning has 2 settled forecasts and could not fill a view; the only
   ways to fill it were decoration or a fabricated statistic. It moved into Chain beside the chain
   state it was learned from.
3. **A duplicated panel.** Chain rendered "Learning" twice, once from ChainPanel and once from the new
   panel. Two panels showing the same numbers is worse than either alone: a reader cannot tell which
   is authoritative and a divergence between them would be invisible.
4. **A hidden cap.** The transactions list did `.slice(0, 12)` for a layout that no longer existed and
   the header said "12 shown" while **39** transactions existed. A reader counting the agent's onchain
   activity on screen would have undercounted it by two thirds. That was a correctness defect found by
   a density measurement.
5. **Columns wider than their content.** `minmax(0,1fr)` holding a 12-character reason name left a
   248x848 band in the ledger; a label column of 21rem holding 44 characters left 64x848. The fix was
   to give the flexible width to the engine's FULL refusal sentence, which is the longest and most
   informative field in the row.
6. **Vertical waste exposed by fixing horizontal waste.** Making the metrics rows two-up halved the
   height the panel needs, and leaving it on `flex-1` opened an 824x384 void beneath it. The panel that
   fills gets the flexible height.

Not one of those six is decoration. Four of them are defects the metric found: a hidden cap that
understated real activity, a duplicated panel, and two cases of the UI holding data it did not show.

## The single-page gate, kept for the record, and the reason it is superseded

5.92% against a 3.9% baseline. Stated plainly rather than by adjusting the metric.

The void moved to 1352,696, 568x216, and it is now inside the **Learning** panel, which holds one
signal statistic and one open forecast because the learning layer has settled exactly **2**
forecasts so far. That panel fills as forecasts settle, which is task 8.5's work, not a layout
change.

What was deliberately NOT done to close the gap, at either 8.10% or 5.92%:

- No decorative panel, sparkline or hero number. "Oversized cards with three numbers" and
  "placeholder charts" are named failure conditions for this project, and inventing content to win a
  density metric would fail the intent while passing the number.
- No shrinking of a panel to hide a void. The panel that grows with real runs keeps the flexible
  height; the alternative is a layout that looks better on thin data and worse on real data, which
  is precisely backwards.

## What the measurement also confirms

- **321 numeric cells** all rendered with `tabular-nums` through the `.num` utility, which is the
  pattern from `evidence/ui-study.md` section 2 (`orderbook-row.tsx:34`). No column jitters on
  update.
- **46.63% ink coverage** at a 12px/16px data-row scale. For comparison, the type scale is the one
  HypeTerminal uses for its order book rows (`packages/ui/src/globals.css:42-45`), one step below
  stock Tailwind, which is what makes this coverage reachable at all.
- Seven panels in one screen with no scrolling of the page body. Only panel interiors scroll, which
  is the `flex-1 min-h-0` rule from `main-workspace.tsx:36,45,48`.
